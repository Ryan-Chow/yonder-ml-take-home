## Data-collection priorities

Ordered by expected value per labelling hour, using the train hard examples above and the val errors in `results/errors.md`. The point is to collect *specific* data for the failure modes we can see, not more of what the model already handles (most medium-sized, single-object outdoor shots are already easy).

1. **Fix labels before collecting anything.** 2 train images have confident detections of objects that carry no label, and the val error analysis found mallets labelled as bottles (`t_bot_mal578`, `t_bot_mal581`). A wrong label teaches the wrong thing no matter how much new data is added. Cost: minutes. Re-check the label-suspect list and every wrong-class error image.
2. **Small, distant objects.** Images whose smallest object is under 32 px are hard 21% of the time (112 images) vs 2% for 32–96 px. On the rover, the mallet has to be found *before* the rover is close, so this is the operating regime that matters. Collect: the mallet and bottles placed 3–15 m from a rover-height camera, on the competition-like terrain (dirt, gravel, scrub), several headings. Consider also training at imgsz 640 for small objects (see `results/efficiency.md` for the latency cost).
3. **Scenes with several objects.** Images with both classes are hard 24% of the time (59 images) vs 3% for single-class images. Part of this is missing labels (priority 1), the rest is crowding and small size. Collect: multi-object scenes with distractors (other bottles, orange tools, cans), and label *every* instance.
4. **Exposure extremes and unseen contexts (from the val errors).** 14 of the val misses are in dark images and 5 in bright ones; by source the most misses come from `green_nalgene_vid_mp` 12, `IMG` 9, `numeric/other` 4. Collect: dawn/dusk, harsh midday sun with hard shadows, backlit shots, and the objects partly occluded (held, half-buried, behind scrub), all at rover height.
5. **Hard negatives.** Most val false positives come from `numeric/other` 9, `green_nalgene_vid_mp` 7, `IMG` 6. Collect rover-terrain images *without* the objects but with look-alikes (blue barrels/cans, orange debris, shadows) and add them as background images (empty label files) so the model learns what is *not* a bottle.
6. **De-prioritise / drop off-domain data.** Product-page screenshots, white-background collages (`Untitled-drawing`) and studio product shots are a web-scrape domain the rover will never see; their errors (tiny thumbnails, colour swatches, extreme close-ups) are not worth fixing with more of the same. Measure the val score without them before deciding to remove them from training.
7. **Close the loop (active learning).** Run the model over unlabelled rover footage and send for labelling the frames where (a) max confidence falls in the uncertain band 0.2–0.6, or (b) a detection flickers on and off between neighbouring frames (`scripts/video_test.py` measures this). After each labelled batch, re-run `scripts/hard_examples.py` and `scripts/error_analysis.py` and re-rank these priorities. Always add new data by *session* to train or val, never split a session (the leakage lesson from `results/audit.md`).
